Envoy structures your work around a hierarchy of objects that mirror how modern teams actually operate. At the top sits your **Organization**, which acts as the account-level container for everything your company does in Envoy. Inside an organization you create **Workspaces** for individual teams or projects, invite **Members** and assign them **Roles**, and connect **Integrations** that pull in the tools your teams already use. Understanding how these objects relate to one another is the fastest path to getting the most out of Envoy.

## Object hierarchy

Here is how Envoy's core objects nest inside one another:

- **Organization** — the top-level account that owns all billing, settings, and data
  - **Workspaces** — scoped environments for a team, project, or product area
    - **Members** — users invited into a workspace with a specific role
      - **Integrations** — third-party connections available within a workspace

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Organization** | The top-level account container that holds all workspaces, members, billing, and settings. |
| **Workspace** | A scoped environment inside an organization used to separate teams, projects, or environments. |
| **Member** | A user who has been invited to an organization or workspace and assigned a role. |
| **Role** | A named set of permissions (Admin, Member, or Viewer) that controls what a member can see and do. |
| **Integration** | A connection between Envoy and a third-party tool such as Slack, GitHub, or Salesforce. |
| **API Key** | A secret credential used to authenticate programmatic requests to the Envoy REST API. |
| **Webhook** | An HTTP callback that Envoy sends to a URL you specify when a specified event occurs. |
